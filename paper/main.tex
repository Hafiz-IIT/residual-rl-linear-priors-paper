\begin{abstract}
This manuscript develops an experimental question: can a learned residual policy improve a transparent linear control prior while retaining inspectable baseline behavior? The accompanying software currently implements only a deterministic scalar residual-control prototype. It does not yet implement a trained reinforcement-learning agent or establish empirical superiority. We specify the proposed architecture, evaluation design, and evidence required before making performance claims.
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}
Residual formulations combine conventional feedback with an RL residual, as demonstrated in robot manipulation by Johannink et al. \cite{johannink2019residual}. Safety and risk constraints require separate treatment; see García and Fernández \cite{garcia2015safe} and Berkenkamp et al. \cite{berkenkamp2017safe}.,The residual-control hypothesis is testable, but the current artifact is an initial deterministic scaffold.
Robotic control often combines structured controllers with data-driven adaptation. A residual formulation preserves a nominal controller and assigns a learned component the narrower task of correcting its action. This can improve interpretability of the baseline, but does not by itself guarantee stability or safety.
\section{Research question and hypotheses}
The primary question is whether a residual policy can reduce task cost relative to a matched nominal controller under controlled model mismatch. H1: a trained residual policy may improve finite-horizon cost on held-out disturbances. H2: constraining residual magnitude may reduce extreme actions, while potentially limiting adaptation. Both hypotheses require experiments.
\section{Proposed method}
Let the nominal action be $u_p(x)$ and the learned residual be $\pi_\theta(x)$. The combined action is
\[
u(x)=\operatorname{clip}(u_p(x)+\operatorname{clip}(\pi_\theta(x),-\rho,\rho),-u_{\max},u_{\max}).
\]
The current companion prototype substitutes a fixed cubic residual for $\pi_\theta$ and is therefore a control scaffold, not an RL implementation.
\section{Experimental design}
Compare nominal-only, residual-only diagnostic, and hybrid policies on matched initial states and disturbance sequences. Include model mismatch, observation noise, action saturation, and held-out parameter settings. Report cumulative cost, tracking error, peak state magnitude, saturation frequency, and failures.
\section{Results}
No empirical results are reported in this draft. This section must be populated only after experiments are executed, logged, and independently reproducible.
\section{Threats to validity}
A scalar synthetic plant cannot establish robotic generalization. Hyperparameter selection, simulator fidelity, reward design, and disturbance coverage may bias results. Bounded actions do not establish closed-loop stability.
\section{Conclusion}
The residual-control hypothesis is testable, but the current artifact is an initial deterministic scaffold. Claims about learned RL performance are deferred until a trained policy and controlled comparisons exist.
